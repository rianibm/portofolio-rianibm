## A little context

Indopaket 3PL is an internal logistics platform for land and air shipment operations, used by operational teams and logistics partners. Teams use it to check shipment status, review tracking history, and access operational reports.

**My part:** Frontend architecture, operational interfaces, form and workflow design, error and empty states, role-aware navigation, data integration, and testing setup.

**Collaboration:** I worked with backend engineers, Product, and Operations teams to translate operational requirements into usable interfaces.

I’ve simplified the workflows and roles here to keep internal information private.

## What I delivered

**A production frontend with separate land and air workflows, role-aware navigation, and shared validation.** Operators can work through shipment stages with actions tied to their role and the current stage, alongside tracking history and loading, empty, and error states.

## Three decisions behind the operational UI

### 1. Separate the workflows that behave differently

Land and air shipments follow different sequences, and some information arrives after work starts. I kept their interface paths separate so each could reflect its own shipment lifecycle.

**Trade-off:** Separate paths create more UI to maintain. I shared validation and domain logic underneath, keeping common rules consistent without flattening the operational differences.

### 2. Tie actions to stage and responsibility

An action can be valid for one stage or role and irrelevant for another. I used stage-dependent actions and role-aware navigation to reflect what an operator can do at that point in the workflow.

**Design consideration:** Hiding or withholding an action can leave someone unsure why it is unavailable. The next validation step is to observe those moments and check whether the interface provides enough context. Role-aware UI also needs backend authorization; navigation alone is not an access-control boundary.

### 3. Make incomplete data part of the interface

Operational screens need to handle records that are loading, missing, or unavailable as well as populated results. I included loading, empty, and error states, with shared validation for form-heavy workflows.

**Trade-off:** These states add implementation and verification work. Treating them as part of the core workflow gives the interface a defined response when data is not ready.

## Testing Strategy

I set up unit and component tests with **Vitest**, **Testing Library**, and **jsdom**, alongside **ESLint**, **Prettier**, and lint-staged for routine code checks.

The testing priorities are form validation, state-dependent actions, and loading, empty, and error states—the interactions that help operators understand what they can do next.

## Outcome and evidence limits

The platform brings together land and air workflows, role-aware navigation, tracking history, and shared validation. Actions follow the shipment stage, so the interface can show what’s available next.

The testing priorities are form validation and interface states. I don’t have operational error rates, task-time comparisons, or usability-test results to share here.

Next, I’d like to observe operators working through both shipment types, especially when information is missing or an action isn’t available yet. Does the screen explain enough? Or does someone still need to ask a colleague? Those moments would tell me where to focus.

## Learnings

This was my first deep dive into 3PL operations. It made me pay much closer attention to what a screen lets someone do, and when. A button can look perfectly fine and still be the wrong next step. That lesson stayed with me.
